ASA

Advanced Security Administration — a separately-priced add-on on top of Enterprise, for more precise control over Login, Secrets and Compliance.

⠿

Think of it this way: Postman's default is already secure. ASA (Advanced Security Administration) is a separately-priced add-on, on top of Enterprise, for organizations that need an extra, more precise layer of control — who logs in from where, whether Secrets are leaking, and who actually holds the encryption key.

Domain Capture
Automatically routes logins by domain, so every employee lands in the right organizational account, not a personal one
Secret Scanner — Dashboard & Alerts
Not just a single scanner, but an org-wide picture: how many exposure attempts, where, and who was involved
Vault Integrations
Azure Key Vault, HashiCorp, AWS Secrets Manager, 1Password — Postman works with infrastructure you already have
BYOK Encryption
The customer holds their own encryption key — Postman never has access to it, at any stage

In one sentence: ASA doesn't replace Postman's baseline security — it adds a layer of control, visibility and compliance at Enterprise scale, for organizations with strict regulatory requirements (SOC 2, ISO, PCI).

⠿
From here, move to the problem

In a large organization, not every employee necessarily logs into the right corporate account — there are "shadow" personal accounts, and nobody really knows how many. Secrets can leak into Repos and Logs, and sometimes the organization only finds out after the fact. And in regulated organizations, "we're secure" isn't enough — you need to prove it with evidence, including who actually holds the encryption key.

Discovery questions — including the two key questions
⠿
First scenario: an insurance company
An insurance company with a compliance requirement to separate accounts

The Compliance team requires full separation between personal Postman accounts and managed organizational accounts — and full tracking of every Secret-exposure attempt, not just "we have a scanner."

Domain Capture automatically routes anyone who signs up with the company email into the managed account, and Secret Scanner gives an organization-wide picture, not just per-workspace.

Who to target: CISO/Compliance (the Lead), IT Admin (execution).

Discovery question to close the scenario"Do you have a way today to know for certain how many personal Postman accounts exist in your organization, off the radar?"
⠿
Second scenario: a credit card company
A credit card company with a PCI-DSS BYOK requirement

Under PCI-DSS, there's sometimes an explicit requirement that the company itself, not an outside vendor, holds the encryption key for sensitive data.

With BYOK, the company keeps the encryption key in its own Vault — and Postman simply can't access the data without that key, even if it wanted to.

Who to target: CISO/Security Architecture (the Lead), Compliance (the PCI requirement).

⠿
Third scenario: a bank
A bank that needs full evidence for a regulatory audit

A banking regulator doesn't settle for "we're secure" — it wants proof: who logged in from where, were there any Secret-exposure attempts, and who holds the encryption keys. ASA gives all three in one place, ready to present to an audit.

Who to target: CISO (the Lead), Internal Audit/Compliance (receives the reports).

  • How many personal Postman accounts do you estimate exist in your organization, off the radar?
    If there's no answer, that's the signal itself.
  • If a regulator asked tomorrow for proof that your encryption key was never held by Postman, do you have that ready?
  • Do you have a central view today of how many Postman accounts exist across the organization, including personal ones?
  • Is there a regulatory requirement for BYOK, or to hold your own encryption key?
  • Have you had cases of Secrets leaking into Repos or Logs?

Common objections

"Isn't Postman's baseline security enough?"
AEFor most organizations, yes. ASA is an extra layer, for Enterprise orgs with specific regulatory requirements — not a fix for a gap.
"Is this an extra charge on top of Enterprise?"
AECorrect, and it's important you know that upfront — it's a separate add-on, not a bundled feature.
"We're not regulated, I don't think this is relevant"
AEFair — ASA is mainly worth it for organizations with SOC 2/ISO/PCI, or a concrete concern about shadow accounts. If that's not you, base Enterprise is likely enough.
