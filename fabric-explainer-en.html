Fabric

Fabric is the organization's central control layer for the AI world — Models, Agents, MCP Servers and internal APIs, all under one roof.

⠿

Think of it this way: the more teams start building AI Agents and connecting to different models — OpenAI, Claude, Gemini — and to MCP servers and internal APIs, the more each team starts managing its own connections, permissions and Credentials on its own. Fabric takes all of that traffic and routes it through a single control point — giving the organization visibility into where AI requests are going, which models and APIs they can reach, how secrets are managed, what it costs, and what actually happened — all under one unified governance layer.

Routing & Reliability
Routes requests between models, automatic fallback when a model goes down, picks the right model for each request
Security & Credentials
The Agent never needs to see or hold the keys itself
Agent Governance
Decide exactly which APIs, tools or MCP Servers each Agent is authorized to reach
Cost & Audit
See who's consuming how many tokens at what cost, and a full record of who ran which Agent, on which model, against which API, and which policy fired

In one sentence: Fabric is the central control layer that connects AI models, Agents, MCP servers and APIs — giving you control over traffic, security, permissions, cost and audit, instead of managing each one separately.

⠿
From here, move to the problem

Say one team works with OpenAI, another with Claude, and an Agent connects to an MCP server — and they all need access to internal systems. Today each of them holds its own keys, permissions and logs, and there's no central layer that sees all the traffic. That's not just a technical problem — it's Shadow AI: the organization doesn't actually know who's talking to whom, at what cost, or under what authority.

Discovery questions — including the two key questions
⠿
First scenario: an insurance company
An insurance company with claims-handling Agents

AI Agents review claims and touch sensitive internal APIs (policies, customers, claims history) — sometimes several Agents across several different models, each with its own keys and permissions.

The problem: if an Agent "misbehaves" or accidentally accesses data it shouldn't (a customer's PII), there's no central way to block it, see who's responsible, or show a regulator exactly what happened. Fabric gives a full audit trail — who ran the Agent, on which model, which API it touched, and which policy allowed or blocked the request.

Who to target: Head of AI/Platform Engineering (the Lead), CISO/Compliance (involved early because of the PII).

Discovery question to close the scenario"If an Agent accesses a customer's sensitive data right now, do you have a way to know that in real time, or do you only find out at the next audit?"
⠿
Second scenario: a credit card company
A credit card company with fraud-detection Agents

The Fraud team runs Agents that use several models in parallel (say, GPT for text analysis, Claude for pattern detection) to raise accuracy, lower false positives, and avoid depending on a single vendor if something breaks.

The problem: managing fallback between models by hand is messy, and nobody actually knows how many tokens each fraud check costs — and at millions of checks a month, that's a real dollar figure. Fabric gives automatic routing and fallback between models, and a full cost picture by team or use case.

Who to target: VP Engineering / Head of Fraud & Risk (the Lead), FinOps/Platform (cost control).

⠿
Third scenario: a bank
A bank with dozens of internal dev teams and Agents

At a large bank, dozens of dev teams each run an Agent or two, each independently deciding which model to use and which internal APIs to connect to. There's no single organizational picture.

The problem: banking regulation (things like SOC 2 / ISO / Model Risk requirements) demands a full audit trail for any AI action touching customer data — not "roughly know," but exact proof. Fabric gives that one unified governance layer across every team, without blocking any team from picking the model that fits them.

Who to target: Head of AI Platform / Architecture (the Lead), Model Risk / Compliance (a critical partner).

Discovery Questions

  • How much are you actually spending on token usage today, and who in the organization sees that number?
    At most organizations the honest answer is "nobody knows exactly" — that's the gap Fabric closes.
  • If an Agent accesses a sensitive internal API right now, do you have a full audit trail on that, in real time?
  • How many teams are building or running AI applications or Agents today, and how is that managed?
  • How do your Agents access LLMs, MCP servers and internal APIs today?
  • As AI usage grows, are there concerns about security, Agent access, Credentials, Governance or costs?
  • Are you using more than one AI model? How do you decide which model gets which request?
Start with agents, then governance
  • Open with: "Are you building agents or AI-powered workflows internally, in your product, or both?"
  • Real traffic — What are agents calling? APIs, tools, MCP servers, LLMs, data, workflows?
  • Control — Who controls access? Central policy or team-by-team decisions?
  • Credentials — Where do credentials live? Centralized or scattered across apps, teams, and agents?
  • Visibility / proof — Can you audit what happened? Who called what, was it allowed, what did it cost?
  • Follow with: "How are you governing what they can access, do, and prove?"

Common objections

"We just use ChatGPT/Copilot"
AEThen Fabric is less critical right now — it becomes relevant once there's more than one Agent/model, MCP Servers, or Agents touching internal APIs.
"We already have an LLM Gateway"
AEA regular gateway routes requests to a model. Fabric adds a governance layer on top for the Agents themselves — who's allowed to reach which API/tool, not just which model.
"We're not building Agents yet"
AEThat's fine — it's worth knowing about early, because in most organizations this happens faster than expected, usually with no governance in place yet.
